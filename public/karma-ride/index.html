Karma Ride

Fair allocation of city-center access to ride-hailing drivers. Without money.

During rush hours, ride-hailing drivers may serve the city center only if they hold a permit, and only a fixed number of permits exist. They are allocated with Karma: a non-tradable credit. Not money, not luck.

01 · The policy

Draw a cordon. Cap the supply.

Ride-hailing drivers gravitate downtown, where requests are dense and trips chain quickly. The result: too many drivers inside the center, too few outside it. Our answer is a cordon: during rush hours, only drivers holding a permit may serve trips that touch the center, and the number of permits is capped at a quota N.

Effect 01

Calmer center

Fewer ride-hailing vehicles downtown: less congestion, faster trips.

Effect 02

Served periphery

Freed supply relocates outward to underserved regions.

Effect 03

Shaped demand

Scarcer downtown rides nudge short trips toward public transport.

The cordon works. But it creates a scarce resource: the permit. Who gets it decides whether the policy is fair.

02 · Allocation

Who gets a permit?

Money?

Wealth wins

Auctions favor the ability to pay. Outside wealth buys priority.

Lottery?

Luck decides

Fair only ex ante: repeated bad luck happens, and luck ignores who needs it today.

Rotation?

Blind turns

Everyone gets a turn, but your day may be exactly the day you cannot drive.

We want fairness over time, immunity to outside wealth, and responsiveness to daily needs. That is what Karma is for.

The currency

Karma: bids without money.

Every driver holds a balance of Karma points (KP): non-tradable, worthless outside the mechanism. Each evening, drivers bid Karma for tomorrow's permits.

STEP 1

Bid

Bid Karma for tomorrow, up to your balance. Not driving? Bid zero.

STEP 2

Allocate

The highest N bidders win the N permits.

STEP 3

Pay

Winners all pay the same price: the lowest winning bid.

STEP 4

Redistribute

Collected Karma returns equally to all drivers. Nothing enters, nothing leaves.

  • A and B hold Monday's permits; both pay the lowest winning bid, 9 KP.
  • The 18 KP collected go back to everyone: 6 KP each, including C.
  • C's balance grew, and their bid will weigh more next time.

Karma is conserved by design. Yielding today literally becomes priority tomorrow.

The drivers

Three drivers, three evenings.

The same rule reads differently depending on the day you are having. Karma turns private schedules and urgency into honest daily priorities.

03 · Next step

The daily-basis simulation.

The rush-hour prototype treats the peak in isolation. But getting the permit or not changes far more than the cordon hours: it changes whether a driver works at all, when they start, and for how long. The next step is an agent-based simulation on a daily basis.

Agent-based modeling.

Imagine you are a driver: how would you decide when to work?

To represent these factors, we summarize each agent by two quantities. A target daily income T sets how long they want to work: missing it hurts about twice as much as exceeding it helps. An hourly reservation wage r(h) sets when: the agent works an hour only if the expected hourly earnings beat r(h), so the curve encodes both the preferences and the constraints. Day-to-day shifts of both (a sick child, a due bill) follow a Markov process, and the simulation categorizes drivers into a finite set of types.

Equilibrium computation.

B1

The daily schedule

Work tomorrow? When, and for how long?

B2

The Karma bid

How much Karma to bid for tomorrow's permit?

Neither behavior is chosen in a vacuum. How others schedule their work shapes the opportunities, and hence the hourly income: if everyone floods the evening peak, peak earnings collapse. And how others bid shapes the chance of winning a permit.

The two behaviors are also coupled to each other. The hourly income shape of the day sets how much a driver wants the permit, and getting the permit or not reshapes how they schedule the day. We therefore model the system as a population game and compute its equilibrium iteratively, with a coupled loop of evolutionary dynamics.

Simulating a full day for every candidate policy would be hopeless, so we split the problem:

SIMULATE ONCERun the day simulator on a space-filling set of supply mixtures, offline.
IDENTIFYFit an hourly earnings map: net rate vs. supply, by permit state.
ITERATESolve the fixed point on the map with damped logit responses, in milliseconds.
VERIFYRerun the true simulator at the solution and certify the prediction.

In the loop, z is the daily state, k the Karma balance, p the permit state, a the work plan and b the bid. The damped logit best response is the evolutionary dynamic: it iterates on the identified earnings map to a fixed point, certified by a full simulator rerun. The unrestricted and lottery variants already run and validate; the Karma-coupled version is what we are building now.
